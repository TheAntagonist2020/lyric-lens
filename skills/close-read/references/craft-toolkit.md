# Craft toolkit

Use these terms when they explain something. Never list a device only to show that it is there. Each entry says what to look for and the question it answers.

## Figures

- **Sustained (extended) metaphor:** one comparison carried across lines or sections. Ask: where does it start, where does it strain, and does the song drop it or turn it?
- **Conceit:** a surprising, worked-out comparison between unlike things. Ask: what does the strangeness do?
- **Metonymy / synecdoche:** a thing named by something linked to it ("the crown" for power) or by a part of it ("hands" for workers). Ask: what does the chosen part reveal?
- **Irony:** the words say one thing and the situation says another. Ask: does the speaker know?
- **Apostrophe:** speech to someone absent, dead, or not human. Ask: why can the addressee not answer?
- **Double / triple entendre:** one phrase that carries 2 or 3 live meanings. Ask: does the rhyme, the placement, or the delivery point to both meanings?
- **Allusion and interpolation:** a reference to another text, song, or event, or a reworked line from another song. Ask: what does the source bring with it?

## Sound

- **Rhyme types:** perfect, slant (near), internal (inside the line), multisyllabic (several syllables rhyme: "invitation / imitation"), mosaic (one word rhymes with several: "orange / door hinge"). Ask: where does the rhyme get denser or looser, and why there?
- **Assonance, consonance, alliteration:** repeated vowels, consonants, or first sounds. Ask: what does the sound tie together?
- **Cadence and stress:** where the beats fall in the line. Ask: which word does the rhythm push forward, and is it the expected word?

## Form

- **Enjambment:** a sentence that runs past the end of the line. Ask: what does the line mean on its own before the next line changes it?
- **Caesura:** a hard pause inside a line. Ask: what does the break isolate?
- **Refrain drift:** a hook that repeats with a small change, or the same words in a new context. Ask: what does the hook mean the last time?
- **The turn (volta):** the place where the song changes its mind. Often the bridge, sometimes the last verse. Ask: what does the song know after the turn that it did not know before?
- **Line length:** a sudden short or long line. Ask: what breaks the pattern here?

## Voice

- **Persona:** a character the singer plays. Do not read it as autobiography without a source.
- **Addressee:** the "you". It can change between sections, or be more than one person.
- **Pronoun and tense shifts:** I to we, you to they, past to present. Ask: what moves in time or in distance?
- **Unreliable speaker:** a speaker whose account the song undercuts. Ask: which lines give the speaker away?
- **Code-switching:** a change of dialect, language, or register. Ask: who is the speaker performing for in that moment?

## Genre notes

- **Hip-hop:** rhyme density and multisyllabic chains, flow switches (a change in rhythm or pocket often marks a change in stance), punchlines and wordplay, ad-libs as a second voice that comments, samples and interpolations as part of the meaning, references that reward local knowledge. Wordplay is the argument, not decoration.
- **Country and Americana:** narrative, concrete objects, and the turn: the hook that means something new after the last verse. Track the story's time jumps.
- **Pop:** repetition is the engine. Track how context changes the hook. Simple words can hide a complex situation. Production and delivery often carry the irony.
- **R&B and soul:** the gap between the words and the delivery (melisma, restraint, a break in the voice). Ask the user what they hear.
- **Folk and protest:** the singer's position as witness. Track the "we", the named places and names, and the call to the listener.
- **Rock, punk, and metal:** slogan and rhetoric, the shouted line, the gap between a bright sound and a dark text (or the other way round).
- **Musical theatre and concept albums:** a character in a story. Read each song for what it does in the larger arc.
